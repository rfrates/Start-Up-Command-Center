## New Pitch

**Inquiry:** 

How can an AI design assistant help emerging musicians translate a song’s mood into album art while keeping their creative authorship, and how does that co-creation affect the emotional accuracy of the result?

**Claude's suggestion:**

Expand the concept

**Define the user.** Pick one specific musician: for example, a home-recording indie or folk artist with a first release and no budget. A specific persona makes your research and critique much stronger.

**Design the workflow as a co-creation process:**

1. **Intake:** the artist shares the song, three mood words, and a few reference images they like.

2. **Options:** the assistant proposes several directions, each with an explanation of its color, type, and imagery choices.

3. **Artist direction:** the artist picks, rejects, and edits, with the assistant explaining what each change does to the mood.

4. **Export:** the assistant produces a print-ready and streaming-ready version. Many distributors ask for square artwork, commonly 3000 × 3000 pixels, so check current requirements before you state them.

Research and testing

- **Authorship:** Do artists feel the final cover is theirs? Ask them directly before and after using the tool.

- **Accuracy:** Use the same viewer mood test from your earlier plan.

- **Time and cost:** Record how long each cover takes compared with hiring a designer or designing alone.

- **Prototype:** A clickable Figma or Milanote mockup of the intake and options screens would be enough for a semester project. You don’t need a working AI system.

Risks to address in your critique

- **Homogenized style:** many AI tools produce similar “moody” aesthetics. Your originality criterion is the right way to test this.

- **Ownership and training data:** be clear about which model you’d use and whether artists’ work is used to train it.

- **Disclosure:** should the cover credit AI assistance? Include this as a research question, not just a footnote.

## Source 2

- **Title and Creator**

    - The Emotional Algorithm: Can AI Understand the Feel of a Song? by Pitch Innovations


- **Link**

    - https://pitchinnovations.com/blog/the-emotional-algorithm-can-ai-understand-the-feel-of-a-song/

- **Type**

    - Online article

- **Reflection**

    - This article talked about whether or not ai is actually able to interpret music or if it is making its judgements based on patterns that people associate with certain feelings. Ways to keep the AI creative

1. **Use qualities instead of objects.** Replace "two people holding hands" with "a feeling of closeness and warmth." Words like closeness, distance, scale, light, stillness, and motion guide the mood without picking the subject.

2. **Say outright that the subject is open.** Add a line like "Choose your own subject or scene. Interpret the mood in an unexpected way."

3. **Ask for concepts before images.** First ask for three different visual concepts with different subjects, then pick one to generate. This stops the AI from settling on the first obvious idea.

4. **Let the avoid list do the steering.** Blocking the clichés pushes the AI toward fresher ideas without you prescribing anything.

5. **Skip examples.** Even "for example, a mug in morning light" tends to get copied.

Example prompt

> Design an album cover for a song called "Kitchen Light." The mood is tender: gentle, intimate, quiet, and safe.
>
> Use this palette: blush, cream, and soft peach, with low saturation. The image should feel close and warm, with soft edges, gentle light, and stillness.
>
> Choose your own subject or scene. Interpret the mood in an unexpected way rather than the most obvious one. Avoid hearts, roses, sunsets, and sparkles. No text on the cover.

## Inquiry Question and Follow Ups

If AI can't feel music, can a designer-made mood card system help it translate a song's emotion into album art listeners recognize?

**Follow Ups-**

1. Do AI-generated covers match how people actually experience a song, with and without the mood cards?

2. Which design elements (color, composition, imagery, texture) does AI follow well, and which does it ignore?

3. Do the cards make AI covers look less generic and same-y?

**The tension behind it:** AI recognizes patterns linked to emotion, but it doesn't feel anything. The project tests whether human interpretation, captured in a designed system, can fill that gap. The goal isn't to prove whether AI experiences emotion. It's to show whether the covers communicate emotion to people.

## What I Am Studying

I'm studying whether design knowledge can be turned into a tool that steers AI toward emotional accuracy. By the end, I want to be able to answer:
